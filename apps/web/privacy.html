Legal

Privacy policy

Your pages are yours, and so is your data. We collect as little as we can to run aired, we say exactly what it is below, and we never sell it.

The short version

  • You can publish without an account. Then we know almost nothing about you.
  • If you sign in, we keep your GitHub id, username, name and email.
  • If you pay, Link (Stripe) handles your card. We never see the card number.
  • People who open your pages don't need an account. We count views, with the country they came from and the website that sent them. We don't store their IP address.
  • We don't put ads or trackers on your pages, and we don't sell or rent anyone's data.

Who we are

aired is run by M&S CapitalSoft LLC, 312 W 2nd St, Unit #A3467, Casper, Wyoming 82601, United States. We decide how the data on this page is used, which makes us its "controller" under data protection law. Reach us at support@aired.sh.

One exception: publishers decide what goes on their own pages. If a page contains personal data, the person who published it is responsible for it.

What we collect and why

When you publish a page

  • The page itself: the HTML you send and its title. We need it to show the page to people with the link.
  • Page settings: expiry, PIN, view limit, project, size and when you published. We store your PIN as a salted hash, not as you typed it, and check what people enter against that. A PIN set before we started hashing is converted the first time someone enters it correctly. A PIN keeps casual visitors out. It isn't a password, so don't reuse one you use anywhere else.
  • A scrambled copy of your update token, so we can check it when you edit or delete the page. We never keep the token itself.
  • Your account id, if you were signed in, so the page shows up in your dashboard.
  • A one-way hash of your IP address, held in memory only, to enforce publishing limits. It disappears when the limit window resets, after an hour (a day for Pro's daily limit). We never write your IP address to storage.

The web form keeps your page links and update tokens in your browser's local storage, so you can manage those pages later. The CLI keeps them in a file on your computer. They stay on your device until you use them.

When you sign in

You sign in through WorkOS, with an email code, Google or GitHub. WorkOS checks who you are and gives us your email address, whether it's verified, and your name. If you use GitHub, we ask it only for your public profile and email, and use GitHub's answer once to find your existing account. We never get access to your repositories, organizations or gists, we never store your GitHub access, and we never see any password. We keep:

  • If you use GitHub: your GitHub numeric id, so your account survives a username change.
  • Your name (and GitHub username, if you use GitHub), to show in your dashboard.
  • Your primary email, only if it's verified and not a GitHub "noreply" address. Otherwise we store nothing. We use it to recognize your account. We don't send marketing email. If we ever send product news, you'll have to opt in. Link sends your receipts and billing emails.
  • Your projects and the list of pages you own.
  • Which sign-in you used (email code, Google or GitHub), with its verified email if it gave one (same rule as above), so a later sign-in reaches the same account.
  • If you create API keys: a one-way hash of each key (never the key itself), its first few characters so you can tell keys apart, its name, when it was created and last used, and when you revoked it.
  • If you connect an app to aired over MCP (for example a chat assistant): which app, where it receives access, and when you approved it. Its access is stored as hashes.

When you pay for Pro

Link, Stripe's checkout, collects your card details, name, email and billing address on its own checkout page. We never see or store your card number. Stripe sends us back, and we keep:

  • Your Stripe customer id and subscription id.
  • Your plan, whether you pay monthly or yearly, its status, when the current period ends and whether it's set to cancel.

We need these to turn Pro on and off, and to answer questions about a payment.

When you open a page

You never need an account to view a page. When you open one, we:

  • Add one to that page's view count, which the publisher sees.
  • Add one to a site-wide count of views per country. The country comes from Cloudflare, which works it out from your connection (for example "DE"). That count doesn't record which page you opened.
  • Record the view for the publisher's analytics: which page, the day, your country, and the website that sent you, as its name only (for example "slack.com", never the full link). If you typed the link or came from an app that doesn't say, there's no website. Publishers on Pro see totals per day, country and website, never single visits.

We don't store your IP address, your browser or device, or the full address you came from. We don't set a cookie unless you enter a PIN. The analytics record is kept in Cloudflare Analytics Engine for three months, then deleted automatically.

A publisher's page can contain its own scripts, including its own analytics. Those belong to the publisher, not us. We don't add ads or trackers. The only thing we add, on Free pages, is the aired badge.

When you report a page

The form on /abuse sends us the page link, the reason you picked, your description, and your email if you choose to give it. We use it to review the page and, if you asked, to reply. We delete reports after 30 days.

On our own website

We use Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits to aired.sh's own pages, like the homepage, pricing and docs. It sets no cookies and doesn't track you across sites. It records things like which page was viewed, the referring site, your browser and your country. It doesn't run on pages people publish: those pages block it.

Our legal reasons

If you're in the EU or UK, the law asks us to say why we're allowed to use your data:

  • To provide aired to you (a contract): publishing, your account and your Pro plan.
  • Legitimate interests: publishing limits, handling abuse reports, view counts, counting visits to aired.sh's own pages, and keeping aired secure. You can object to these. Write to us and we'll look at your case.
  • Legal obligations: answering lawful requests from authorities.

We don't make automated decisions about you that have legal or similar effects.

Where it's stored

Everything runs on Cloudflare. Pages, and the screenshots of them shown in link previews and on your dashboard, live in Cloudflare R2. Page settings, account records, abuse reports and the records of apps you connect live in Cloudflare KV. Account and sign-in records (including the email you signed in with), API key hashes, billing records and view counts live in Cloudflare D1. The analytics record of each view lives in Cloudflare Analytics Engine. So does a tiny blurred copy of each page's screenshot, shown on your dashboard while the screenshot loads. Cloudflare runs a global network, so your data is processed in many countries, including the United States. Link (Stripe), GitHub and WorkOS are in the United States too. The person who runs aired works from outside the US and the EU, and reaches this data from there. When your data leaves the EU or UK, we rely on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework where the company is certified, and on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses where it isn't.

How long we keep it

  • Pages: until they expire or you delete them. When a page expires, we stop serving it right away and delete its file within 30 days. A page that reaches its view limit stops being served, but we keep it until it expires or you delete it.
  • Deleted pages: removed from storage right away.
  • Your account: until you delete it. Deleting it ends every session (other browsers and CLI logins too), disconnects every app you connected, deletes your API keys, and removes your account record, sign-in records and email. Other sessions stop within about a minute. Your pages stay up as anonymous pages without your name. Delete them first if you want them gone.
  • Billing records: until you delete your account. Then we delete your Stripe ids and plan history too. Link keeps its own record of each sale, under its own policy.
  • API keys: until you delete your account, revoked ones included.
  • Abuse reports: 30 days.
  • Backups: we keep a nightly backup of page and account records for 30 days, and a copy of each expired page's file for 14 days after we delete it, only to recover from an outage or mistake. So something you delete can stay in a backup for up to 30 days before it's gone for good.
  • IP hashes for publishing limits: in memory only, gone within a day at most.
  • View counts: as long as the page exists. The site-wide country tally holds no personal data.
  • View analytics (page, day, country, referring website): three months, then deleted automatically.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, export or delete the data we hold about you. You can also ask us to stop or limit using it. You don't need a reason. Here's how:

  • Delete your account: from your dashboard. There's no undo.
  • Delete a page: from your dashboard, with aired delete <id> in the CLI, or through the API with its update token.
  • Remove a page you lost the link to: use /abuse and pick "I published this and lost the link to manage it."
  • Remove a page someone else published about you: report it on /abuse.
  • Anything else: email support@aired.sh. We reply within 30 days.

If you're in the EU or UK and you're unhappy with how we handled your data, you can complain to your local data protection authority. If you're in California, you have the same rights to know, delete and correct. We don't sell or share personal information as California law defines it.

Children

aired isn't meant for anyone under 16. If you think someone under 16 has given us personal data, email support@aired.sh and we'll delete it.

Security

Every connection to aired uses HTTPS. Session cookies are HttpOnly and Secure. Update tokens are stored only as hashes. Page PINs are stored as salted hashes (older ones once they're next used), and PIN guesses are rate-limited. Sign-in asks for the smallest set of permissions. The code is open source, so anyone can check how it handles data. If we ever have a breach that puts your data at risk, we'll tell affected users and the authorities as the law requires.

Changes and questions

When we change this policy, we update this page and the date at the top. The full history is in the public repository. If a change affects how we use data you already gave us, we tell signed-in users before it takes effect. Questions? Email support@aired.sh. See also our terms and refund policy.
