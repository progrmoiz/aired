Legal

Privacy policy

Your pages are yours, and so is your data. We collect as little as we can to run aired, we say exactly what it is below, and we never sell it.

The short version

  • You can publish without an account. Then we know almost nothing about you.
  • If you sign in, we keep your GitHub id, username, name and email.
  • If you pay, Stripe handles your card. We never see the card number.
  • People who open your pages don't need an account. We count views and the country they came from. We don't store their IP address.
  • We don't put ads or trackers on your pages, and we don't sell or rent anyone's data.

What we collect and why

When you publish a page

  • The page itself: the HTML you send and its title. We need it to show the page to people with the link.
  • Page settings: expiry, PIN, view limit, project, size and when you published. We keep the PIN so we can check it when someone enters it.
  • A scrambled copy of your update token, so we can check it when you edit or delete the page. We never keep the token itself.
  • Your account id, if you were signed in, so the page shows up in your dashboard.
  • A one-way hash of your IP address, held in memory only, to enforce publishing limits. It disappears when the limit window resets, after an hour (a day for Pro's daily limit). We never write your IP address to storage.

The web form keeps your page links and update tokens in your browser's local storage, so you can manage those pages later. The CLI keeps them in a file on your computer. They stay on your device until you use them.

When you sign in

Today you sign in with GitHub. We ask GitHub only for read:user and user:email. We never get access to your repositories, organizations or gists, and we never see your GitHub password. We keep:

  • Your GitHub numeric id, so your account survives a username change.
  • Your GitHub username and display name, to show in your dashboard.
  • Your primary email, only if it's verified and not a GitHub "noreply" address. Otherwise we store nothing. We use it to recognize your account. We don't send marketing email.
  • Your projects and the list of pages you own.

When you pay for Pro

Stripe collects your card details, name, email and billing address on its own checkout page. We never see or store your card number. Stripe sends us back, and we keep:

  • Your Stripe customer id and subscription id.
  • Your plan, whether you pay monthly or yearly, its status, when the current period ends and whether it's set to cancel.

We need these to turn Pro on and off, and to keep the billing records tax law asks for.

When you open a page

You never need an account to view a page. When you open one, we:

  • Add one to that page's view count, which the publisher sees.
  • Add one to a site-wide count of views per country. The country comes from Cloudflare, which works it out from your connection (for example "DE").
  • Keep a list of the 20 most recent views across all of aired, with the page title, the country and the time, so we can see that aired is working.

We don't store your IP address. We don't set a cookie unless you enter a PIN. Pro's view analytics (views over time, countries and referrers) are coming soon. [OWNER TO DECIDE: describe exactly what Pro analytics records, e.g. referrer and where it's stored, before that feature ships]

A publisher's page can contain its own scripts, including its own analytics. Those belong to the publisher, not us. We don't add ads or trackers. The only thing we add, on Free pages, is the aired badge.

When you report a page

The form on /abuse sends us the page link, the reason you picked, your description, and your email if you choose to give it. We use it to review the page and, if you asked, to reply. We delete reports after 30 days.

Our legal reasons

If you're in the EU or UK, the law asks us to say why we're allowed to use your data:

  • To provide aired to you (a contract): publishing, your account and your Pro plan.
  • Legitimate interests: publishing limits, handling abuse reports, view counts and keeping aired secure. You can object to these. Write to us and we'll look at your case.
  • Legal obligations: billing records for tax and accounting, and answering lawful requests from authorities.

We don't make automated decisions about you that have legal or similar effects.

Where it's stored

Everything runs on Cloudflare. Pages live in Cloudflare R2. Page settings, view counts, account records and abuse reports live in Cloudflare KV. Billing records live in Cloudflare D1. Cloudflare runs a global network, so your data is processed in many countries, including the United States. Stripe and GitHub are also based in the United States. Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, export or delete the data we hold about you. You can also ask us to stop or limit using it. You don't need a reason. Here's how:

  • Delete your account: from your dashboard. There's no undo.
  • Delete a page: from your dashboard, with aired delete <id> in the CLI, or through the API with its update token.
  • Remove a page you lost the link to: use /abuse and pick "I published this and lost the link to manage it."
  • Remove a page someone else published about you: report it on /abuse.
  • Anything else: email [OWNER TO DECIDE: privacy contact email]. We reply within 30 days.

If you're in the EU or UK and you're unhappy with how we handled your data, you can complain to your local data protection authority. If you're in California, you have the same rights to know, delete and correct. We don't sell or share personal information as California law defines it.

Security

Every connection to aired uses HTTPS. Session cookies are HttpOnly and Secure. Update tokens are stored only as hashes. GitHub sign-in asks for the smallest set of permissions. The code is open source, so anyone can check how it handles data. If we ever have a breach that puts your data at risk, we'll tell affected users and the authorities as the law requires.
